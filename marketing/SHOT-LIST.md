# Process Film — Shot List & Production Spec

A 30-second brand film shootable by one person, with a phone, for about
$30 in materials.

**Most of it needs no product**, which is the point — seven of the nine
beats are the rice water process itself, so this doubles as the
build-in-public content that grows the waitlist while the formula is
still being tested.

---

## Concept

Mass-market beauty advertising uses the grammar of *transformation*: one
dramatic reveal, before → after.

This uses *repetition*: the same small act, many times, over years. Rice
in water. Water clouding. Steam. Straining. Hands. The same gesture on a
different morning, in different light.

Not a compromise forced by budget — it's the only structure that matches
"nothing here works in a week." See `POSITIONING.md`.

---

## Beat sheet — 30s

| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0–4s | Dry rice grains falling into a glass bowl of water. Macro, 120fps. Silence. |
| 4–9s | Water clouding. Slow swirl, the white bloom through clear water. |
| 9–13s | Steam rising off the pasteurising pot. Soft window backlight. |
| 13–17s | Hands straining through a coffee filter. Real hands, unstyled. |
| 17–21s | Droplets on linen, on ceramic, on the bottle shoulder. |
| 21–25s | Mist crossing a shaft of window light. |
| 25–28s | Product, static, on linen. |
| 28–30s | YUUKI. Then the end card. |

No voiceover. Room tone and water sounds, or a single sustained note.
Silence suits this brand in a way it wouldn't suit a competitor.

## Shot list

### Shootable now — no product required

- Rice falling into water (macro, 120fps)
- Rice water clouding / swirling
- Steam off the pot during pasteurising
- Straining through the coffee filter
- Water beading on linen, ceramic, wood
- Mist through a light shaft
- Hands — pouring, straining, wringing

**Time-sensitive:** these shots only exist while a batch is actively
being made. Shoot during a rice water session, not after.

### Blocked on product + hair

- Bottle on linen with the real label
- Misting damp curls
- Scrunching

## Lighting — one window

North-facing if available; it stays consistent for hours.

| Role | Kit |
|---|---|
| Key | Window, sheer curtain or parchment taped over it as diffusion |
| Bounce | White foam board opposite the window (~$5) |
| Negative fill | Black foam board — deepens shadows, stops it looking flat |
| Backlight for water | Shoot *toward* the window; droplets and steam glow |

**Surfaces matter more than gear.** Raw linen, unglazed ceramic, pale
wood, stone. Match props to the site palette (`--rice #F3EEE2`,
`--kraft #D9CDB4`) and the footage matches the brand for free.

---

## Camera

- Vertical 4K — that's where it lives
- 60fps normal, **120fps** for water inserts (240 is for hair-flip work,
  not this style)
- **Lock exposure and white balance manually.** Auto will hunt and ruin
  slow-motion takes.
- Macro: get physically close, or a ~$15 clip-on macro lens

---

## Grade — DaVinci Resolve (free)

The move that separates this from mass-market beauty grading: **lift the
blacks.** Beauty ads crush contrast for punch; this wants matte and
filmic.

1. Lift shadows so blacks sit slightly grey
2. Desaturate globally ~10%
3. Warm the highlights
4. **Set the white point to `#F3EEE2`, not pure white** — grading toward
   the site cream rather than neutral is the single detail that makes the
   film unmistakably ours

## Budget

Foam board, a linen remnant, coffee filters. ~$30 if a tripod needs

already does that.
